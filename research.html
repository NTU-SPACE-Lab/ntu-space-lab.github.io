Intelligence, grounded in reality.

Our research integrates perception, predictive modeling, and decision-making to investigate how intelligent systems acquire grounded representations of the physical world and use them to act. Four complementary directions structure this agenda.

01 Embodied Intelligence How perception, representation, and action are coupled through interaction. 02 World Models Modeling environment dynamics and the consequences of actions. 03 Spatial Intelligence Reconstructing geometry and modeling physical interaction. 04 Multimodal Intelligence Evidence-grounded understanding and reasoning across modalities.

Embodied Intelligence

Studying how perception, representation, and action are coupled in agents that acquire goal-directed behavior through interaction and adapt to changing environments.

Embodied intelligence studies how intelligent behavior emerges from the coupling of an agent's physical embodiment, perception, and control. Unlike systems evaluated on static data, embodied agents operate in noisy and changing environments, where decisions must be made under partial observability and actions alter the conditions for subsequent perception and control. Our research develops representations and learning methods that connect multimodal observations to goal-directed action, enabling agents to build grounded understanding of their surroundings, reason under uncertainty, and adapt through interaction. A central aim is generalization across tasks, objects, and embodiments, with an emphasis on reliable behavior when sensing, dynamics, or task conditions differ from those encountered during training.

World Models

Learning predictive representations of environment dynamics to anticipate action consequences and inform decisions over extended horizons.

World models learn how environments evolve and how an agent's actions shape future states. Our research develops predictive representations that integrate observations over time, infer unobserved state, and forecast the task-relevant consequences of interventions. Such models serve as internal simulators in which plans can be assessed before execution, reducing reliance on costly physical trial and error. We study how to preserve geometric, physical, and temporal structure over long horizons, and how calibrated uncertainty signals when a prediction can be trusted. The aim is learned simulation that remains reliable for planning, policy improvement, and evaluation under partial observability and distribution shift.

Spatial Intelligence

Learning representations of geometry, appearance, and physical structure that link visual observations to the structure and dynamics of three-dimensional scenes.

Spatial intelligence concerns how models acquire structured representations of the three-dimensional world and its evolution over time. Our research learns from images and video to reconstruct geometry, appearance, and object identity across viewpoints and dynamic scenes, using both implicit neural fields and explicit representations such as 3D Gaussian splatting. We study how these representations support view synthesis, spatial reasoning, and reliable queries about scene structure, and how they can be extended with physical properties and interaction dynamics that are not recoverable from appearance alone. A central concern is how uncertainty in reconstructed geometry and inferred physical parameters affects downstream prediction and decision-making under partial observations.

Multimodal Intelligence

Grounding perception, language, and temporal reasoning in shared evidence for reliable understanding and tool use.

Multimodal intelligence studies how models integrate complementary signals, including vision, language, audio, and spatial representations, into a coherent and evidence-grounded account of the world. Our research develops shared representations that preserve fine-grained visual detail, align events across modalities, and resolve ambiguous references in images and long-form video. We examine how these representations support instruction following, temporal reasoning, and multi-step inference, including settings where agents must seek additional observations or use external tools. A central concern is whether conclusions remain traceable to observable evidence and robust as information accumulates, with calibrated uncertainty and mechanisms for detecting and correcting errors.
